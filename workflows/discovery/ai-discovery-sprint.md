# AI-Assisted Discovery Sprint

**Overview:** Use AI to accelerate the research and problem-framing phase of a design project — from raw inputs to a clear problem statement in hours, not days.

**Tools needed:**
- ChatGPT or Claude (synthesis + analysis)
- Figjam or Miro (affinity mapping)
- Your usual note-taking tool

**Time estimate:** 3–5 hours

**Design phase:** Discovery

---

## Steps

### 1. Dump your raw research
Gather everything you have: interview notes, survey responses, support tickets, stakeholder feedback. Don't organize yet.

### 2. Synthesize with AI
Paste your raw notes into ChatGPT or Claude with this prompt:

```
You are a UX researcher. I'm going to give you raw user research notes.
Identify the top 5 pain points, group them by theme, and suggest a one-sentence problem statement for each theme.

Research notes:
[paste notes here]
```

### 3. Challenge the output
Ask the AI: *"What am I missing? What assumptions am I making?"*  
This surfaces blind spots before they become design decisions.

### 4. Map themes visually
Take the AI-generated themes into Figjam or Miro. Do a quick human affinity map to validate — does the AI's grouping match your gut feel? Adjust as needed.

### 5. Draft a How Might We statement
Use AI to turn your top problem statement into 5 HMW questions:

```
Turn this problem statement into 5 "How Might We" questions at varying levels of ambition — from incremental to transformative.

Problem statement: [your statement here]
```

### 6. Prioritize and commit
Pick one HMW to carry into ideation. Write it on a sticky note and put it somewhere visible.

---

## Tips

- AI is fast at pattern recognition but doesn't understand *context* — always sanity-check its themes against what you actually heard from users.
- Use Claude for longer documents (it handles more tokens); use ChatGPT for back-and-forth iteration.
- Don't skip step 3. Asking the AI what it missed is often the most valuable step.

---

## Output

At the end of this workflow you'll have:
- A set of validated research themes
- A shortlist of HMW questions
- One clear problem statement to carry into ideation
